Analog Function Generator

Function generator capable of producing sine, square, triangular, and sawtooth waveforms with adjustable duty cycle

Project Overview

The Analog Function Generator was developed as the final project for the third-semester laboratory practice module, demonstrating the ability to apply practical electronics knowledge to a complete hardware design. This project delivers a versatile signal source capable of generating sine, triangular, sawtooth, and square waves with an adjustable duty cycle, covering a frequency range from 20 Hz to 20 kHz.

Through the design, construction, and testing phases, this project provided valuable hands-on experience in analog circuit design, signal shaping, and waveform synthesis using operational amplifiers.

Motivation

The motivation behind this project was to bridge theoretical learning with practical circuit implementation, reinforcing core electronics concepts. By building a function generator from the ground up, I deepened my understanding of op-amp behavior, feedback configurations, and waveform generation techniques.

This project also served as a foundation for exploring digital waveform generation in future work, combining analog precision with digital control flexibility.

Key Features

  • Multiple Waveform Generation: Produces sine, square, triangular, and sawtooth waveforms.

  • Adjustable Frequency and Duty Cycle: Frequency range from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, with tunable duty cycle control.

  • Op-Amp Based Design: Utilizes operational amplifiers for waveform shaping and amplitude control.

  • Hands-On Learning: Strengthened practical skills in circuit debugging, signal integrity, and component selection.

  • Educational Impact: Served as a capstone analog project, consolidating essential knowledge in electronics engineering.

Reflections

This project provided an excellent opportunity to apply analog design theory to a real-world circuit. It strengthened my ability to identify appropriate op-amps for specific roles, troubleshoot hardware issues, and ensure waveform accuracy.

Looking forward, I plan to develop a digital function generator, expanding on this foundation to integrate microcontroller-based control and DAC-driven waveform synthesis for higher precision and programmability.

I’m deeply grateful to all team members and mentors who contributed their knowledge and dedication — their collaboration and support made this project a success.
